## Aipaca SMS Communication Opt-In and Consent Information

**Aipaca** is an IRB-approved AI clinical decision-support assistant for lung cancer screening, developed and operated by researchers at the University of Oklahoma. This page describes how established patients at participating University of Oklahoma clinics opt in to receive SMS text messages from Aipaca, what those messages contain, and how to stop them at any time.

### Who operates this SMS service?
The **University of Oklahoma** operates this SMS program under the brand name **Aipaca**. Aipaca is directed by Dr. Chongle Pan of the School of Computer Science and the Department of Microbiology and Plant Biology, along with researcher Yunlong Liu. Aipaca is delivered under signed HIPAA Business Associate Agreements (BAAs) with Amazon Web Services (cloud hosting and SMS delivery via AWS End User Messaging) and OpenAI (the conversational AI engine).

### Who is eligible to opt in?
To opt in to Aipaca SMS, you must be:
- an adult patient (aged 50–80),
- established at a University of Oklahoma primary care or pulmonology clinic that participates in the Aipaca lung cancer screening research protocol, and
- enrolled in the IRB-approved research study after signing the paper consent form during an in-person clinic visit.

### How do patients opt in?
Opt-in is collected **only** via a signed paper IRB consent form during an in-person clinic visit at the University of Oklahoma. Aipaca does **not** collect SMS opt-in via web forms, keywords, verbal-only agreements, QR codes, or third-party brokers.

During the clinic visit:
1. The clinician verbally introduces the Aipaca SMS service.
2. The patient is given the IRB-approved paper consent form, which includes a clearly labeled "**SMS Communication Consent**" section on the same page as the patient's signature.
3. The patient reads the disclosures in the "SMS Communication Consent" section (see next section) and the verbatim consent sentence (see below).
4. The patient handwrites their mobile phone number, writes their initials in the dedicated SMS-consent box (separate from any other consent on the form), signs, and dates the form. Clinic staff co-sign as witness.
5. The signed form is filed as the durable record of the patient's explicit consent. **No SMS is sent from Aipaca to any number without a signed consent record on file.**

### What disclosures does the patient see before consenting?
The "SMS Communication Consent" section of the paper form presents each of the following in writing on the same page as the signature:

- **Program name / brand identification:** Aipaca (a University of Oklahoma IRB-approved lung cancer screening research assistant).
- **Message frequency:** approximately 1–5 messages per patient per consultation. This is a conversational research service — not a recurring or scheduled marketing campaign.
- **Message and data rates may apply.**
- **Opt-out instructions:** Reply **STOP** to opt out at any time (also END, QUIT, CANCEL, UNSUBSCRIBE).
- **Customer care / HELP:** Reply **HELP** to any Aipaca message, or call the University of Oklahoma Office of Research Services at (405) 325-4757.
- **Terms & Conditions:** [https://aipaca.app/terms](https://aipaca.app/terms)
- **Privacy Policy:** [https://aipaca.app/privacy](https://aipaca.app/privacy)
- **No third-party sharing:** The University of Oklahoma does not share, sell, or rent phone numbers to any third party. Text messaging originator opt-in data and consent will not be shared with any third parties.
- **Carrier disclaimer:** Carriers are not liable for delayed or undelivered messages.

### Verbatim SMS consent sentence on the paper form
The consent statement patients read and initial on the paper form reads exactly as follows:

> "I consent to receive SMS text messages from Aipaca (a University of Oklahoma IRB-approved lung cancer screening research assistant) at the mobile phone number I provide. Message frequency is approximately 1–5 messages per consultation. **Message and data rates may apply.** I can opt out at any time by replying STOP. Reply HELP for help. Opting out will not affect my clinical care or my participation in the research study. Terms: https://aipaca.app/terms. Privacy: https://aipaca.app/privacy. My initials here indicate consent: ______"

### What SMS messages will patients receive after opting in?
Once opted in, patients may receive brief conversational SMS messages from Aipaca such as:
- an enrollment confirmation message,
- follow-up questions about smoking history or screening status,
- guidance about next steps to discuss with their primary care clinician,
- reminders about upcoming screening appointments.

An example enrollment confirmation SMS reads:

> "Aipaca: You are now enrolled to receive SMS from Aipaca, a University of Oklahoma lung cancer screening research assistant. Msg freq 1–5 per consultation. Message and data rates may apply. Reply HELP for help or STOP to opt out."

Aipaca is a research service; it does not provide medical diagnosis or treatment and is not a substitute for evaluation by a qualified clinician.

### How do patients withdraw consent (opt out)?
A patient can withdraw consent to receive SMS from Aipaca at any time by replying **STOP** (or END, QUIT, CANCEL, UNSUBSCRIBE) to any Aipaca message. The patient's number is opted out immediately and a single opt-out confirmation SMS is returned. No further messages will be sent. **Opting out of SMS does not affect the patient's clinical care or their participation in the research study.**

Patients may also request opt-out by calling the University of Oklahoma Office of Research Services at (405) 325-4757 or emailing [ris@ou.edu](mailto:ris@ou.edu).

### Privacy and HIPAA
Aipaca operates under the University of Oklahoma's HIPAA-covered entity. All patient phone numbers, SMS content, voice recordings, and transcripts are stored in encrypted, access-controlled storage under the IRB-approved protocol and applicable HIPAA record-retention requirements, and are accessible only to authorized research personnel. Aipaca uses HIPAA-eligible cloud infrastructure and AI services under signed Business Associate Agreements (BAAs) with Amazon Web Services and OpenAI. These providers process protected health information only as permitted by the BAAs and do not use Aipaca data to train models or for their own purposes.

Full privacy policy: [https://aipaca.app/privacy](https://aipaca.app/privacy)  
Full terms & conditions: [https://aipaca.app/terms](https://aipaca.app/terms)
